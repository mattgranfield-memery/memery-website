What happens after the audit

Fix what is actually getting in the way.

GEO isn’t a single trick. Depending on the evidence, the work may sit across search, content, brand clarity, reputation, digital PR and measurement.

01 — Discoverability

Technical + source audit

Indexation, canonical pages, internal linking, crawler access, readable evidence, structured data and the fundamentals that affect retrieval.

02 — Clarity

Entity + proposition clarity

Make your category, audience, geography, expertise and offer easier to understand consistently across the public web.

03 — Evidence

Answerability + content strategy

Create genuinely useful comparisons, constraints, case outcomes, research, expert answers and original data worth citing.

04 — Reputation

Corroboration + earned authority

Strengthen the public record through credible media, partner, customer and specialist validation — not manufactured mentions.

05 — Distribution

Third-party recommendation strategy

Identify where credible independent recommendation signals can be created, strengthened or corrected across the sources AI systems draw on.

06 — Measurement

Ongoing AI visibility monitoring

Retest the same high-value prompts, competitor set, citations and accuracy over time and connect changes to qualified demand.

Case study — Charles Sturt University

Reframing “best university” around the outcome that mattered.

For many “best university” queries, AI answers are strongly shaped by widely cited third-party ranking and comparison sources.

That tends to favour institutions with the strongest research prestige. Charles Sturt’s competitive strength was different: graduate employment outcomes.

Prompt strategy Third-party evidence Brand positioning AI visibility

Rather than trying to out-rank research-intensive universities on their strongest ground, we focused on credible independent evidence supporting the attribute Charles Sturt could genuinely own: helping students graduate into work.

Read the full case study

Global university rankings such as Times Higher Education and QS are highly visible, highly cited sources and can strongly influence how AI systems answer broad “best university” questions.

Their methodologies place substantial weight on research, reputation and international profile — useful measures, but not always the measures prospective students care about most.

We approached the problem differently.

We identified the course categories and customer prompts where employment outcomes, starting salary and practical career prospects were more meaningful decision criteria. We then strengthened the third-party evidence base around those claims and made it easier for AI systems to corroborate them.

The result was a different kind of recommendation. In our testing, AI responses began distinguishing between research prestige and graduate employment outcomes, and surfaced Charles Sturt as a strong choice where getting into the profession was the priority.

The lesson: GEO is not about persuading AI that a brand is “best” in every sense. It is about making sure AI understands what the brand is genuinely best at — and can find credible third-party evidence to support that recommendation.

FAQ

The questions worth asking before you buy GEO.

Can you guarantee we’ll appear in ChatGPT?

No. AI products use different models, retrieval systems and sources, and outputs can vary. We can improve the quality and availability of the signals they rely on, then measure whether visibility changes.

Is GEO just SEO with a new name?

No — but there is significant overlap. Technical search foundations still matter. GEO adds the broader problem of entity clarity, third-party corroboration, citation, answer quality and recommendation behaviour.

Which AI platforms do you test?

We choose the platform mix based on the audience and category rather than treating every AI product as equally important. That may include ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI experiences, Perplexity, Copilot and others.

How quickly can results change?

It varies. Some changes can appear quickly in retrieval-driven answers; others depend on crawling, source adoption, model updates or a broader change in the public evidence about your brand.

Do we need schema, llms.txt or special “AI content”?

Sometimes technical formats are useful, but none is a universal shortcut. We start with the actual visibility problem, the sources influencing answers and the quality of the evidence before prescribing tactics.

How do you measure GEO?

With a repeatable prompt panel and separate metrics for coverage, mentions, citations, recommendation, accuracy and — where possible — qualified demand.
