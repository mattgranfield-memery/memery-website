The real question is not “How can marketing use AI?” It is: what should the marketing function look like when intelligence, analysis and content production are abundant?
The opportunity

Faster copy is useful.
A different operating model is better.

Most marketing teams begin with ChatGPT drafts, meeting summaries and ad variants. That is a sensible start. It is also the least interesting part of the opportunity. The bigger gains come when AI improves decisions, makes personalisation viable, connects to real workflows and gives teams capabilities they could not previously afford.

Where many teams stop

Approved tools. A prompt library. A training day.

Useful, but incomplete. Individual productivity gains do not automatically become organisational capability. Without shared workflows, priorities, governance, measures and ownership, AI stays fragmented and difficult to scale.

Where we help you get

A marketing system designed around AI.

A clear maturity target, prioritised use cases, documented workflows, risk thresholds, human-accountability rules, capability development, technology choices and a realistic sequence for getting from experiment to practice.

Five levels of value

Move beyond “AI makes us faster”.

We use a simple value ladder to help teams distinguish small productivity wins from the applications that can genuinely reshape marketing performance.

01

Assist

Make individuals faster: research, first drafts, editing, summaries and analysis.

02

Automate

Remove repetitive, low-judgement work: tagging, reporting, variants and workflow administration.

03

Augment

Improve decisions: audience analysis, insight generation, scenario testing and next-best-action recommendations.

04

Personalise

Make relevant one-to-one communication viable at a scale that manual marketing cannot support.

05

Transform

Create capabilities that were previously impractical: always-on intelligence, autonomous agents and continuous optimisation.

From playbook to practice

A credible first 90 days.

We favour evidence over enthusiasm: test low-risk workflows, standardise what works, then invest in a small number of strategically important builds.

1–30

Experiment

  • Identify high-volume, genuinely low-risk tasks.
  • Give a small team approved tools and shared workflows.
  • Measure time, quality and staff experience.
  • Keep the failures — they are useful evidence too.
31–60

Industrialise

  • Turn the strongest experiments into documented workflows.
  • Establish governance and risk-tiering processes.
  • Run a first medium-stakes pilot.
  • Measure against a baseline or control where practical.
61–90

Transform

  • Choose two or three genuinely strategic AI projects.
  • Define success before the build starts.
  • Set the audit and review cadence.
  • Move into a properly governed implementation phase.
