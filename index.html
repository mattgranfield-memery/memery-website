What marketing science demands now

Marketing changed. Marketing science didn’t.

The fundamentals are durable: understand the audience, earn attention, build distinctive memories and be easy to retrieve when a buying situation arises.

What has changed is the environment in which those jobs have to happen. Attention is fragmented across short-form feeds, long-form content is creating deeper engagement, and AI is increasingly shaping how people research and choose. The principles haven’t changed. The way we apply them has.

01

Understand

Good marketing starts with knowing who matters and why they choose. Research, segmentation, category entry points, behavioural drivers and customer insight create the foundation. Before you decide what to say, where to say it or how to use AI, you need to understand the audiences that matter and the situations in which your brand needs to come to mind.

02

Earn attention

Attention is harder to win — and easier to lose. Doom-scrolling feeds have compressed the window for getting noticed. Social and video content needs to be distinctive immediately, designed for the channel and strong enough to interrupt habitual scrolling.

03

Build memory

Some of the best opportunities for memory now come from going longer. Podcasts, video and long-form content can hold attention for 30, 60 or 90 minutes. Used well, that sustained exposure builds familiarity, trust and richer memory structures that short-form content rarely can.

04

Be retrievable

Mental availability now has a machine-mediated layer. Customers are increasingly asking AI to research categories, compare options and build shortlists. Brands need to be easy for people to recall — and easy for AI systems to understand, substantiate and recommend.
